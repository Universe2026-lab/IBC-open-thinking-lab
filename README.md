# IBC — Intelligent Beings: Competition and Coordination

## An open lab for thinking

IBC is an **open lab for thinking** about intelligent beings, competition, coordination, and the evolving relationships among human, animal, cellular, and Human–Machine Hybrid Intelligence (HMHI).

This is **not a finished theory**, not an SOP, and not a framework we are trying to own. It is a working question space intended to invite others to explore, criticize, extend, reshape, or replace parts of the current model.

> **Protect the question, not the proposal.**

> **Explore the process, not just the result.**

> **Preserve uncertainty rather than forcing closure.**

## Why IBC?

Public discussion often emphasizes competition:

- human vs. AI
- company vs. company
- nation vs. nation
- job vs. automation
- one model vs. another

IBC asks us to see **competition and coordination together**.

\[
\text{Future of intelligent systems}
=
F(\text{Competition},\text{Coordination},\ldots)
\]

The working idea is that different intelligent beings may occupy different regions, or metaphorical “wavelengths,” of a broader intelligence spectrum. Their future interactions may involve specialization, overlap, competition, coordination, and emergent collective capability rather than simple replacement.

## Working categories

The present framework uses:

\[
\mathcal B
=
\{
B_{\text{human}},
B_{\text{animal}},
B_{\text{cellular}},
B_{\text{HMHI}},
R
\}
\]

where \(R\) preserves unnamed, uncovered, or currently unknown forms of intelligence.

For present AI systems we provisionally use:

**HMHI — Human–Machine Hybrid Intelligence**

The term is not treated as final.

## How to participate

You do not need to agree with IBC.

Useful contributions include:

- identifying hidden assumptions;
- proposing better definitions;
- challenging equations;
- suggesting missing dimensions;
- providing counterexamples;
- adding cases from biology, R&D, economics, society, or technology;
- proposing alternative system boundaries;
- expanding the residual \(R\);
- showing where the framework fails.

If the project causes someone to ask a question they had not previously considered, it is already useful.

## Origin

This exploration was initiated through an extended dialogue between a human contributor and ChatGPT. Questions, analogies, disagreements, mathematical representations, revisions, and new concepts emerged iteratively through the interaction.

The process itself is part of the project.
