## 2. How the thinking evolved

An early personal aspiration was: **“I have a dream: I want our kids to fly to explore the universe.”** The phrase “our kids” was used metaphorically for human-created machine intelligence, not as a claim that present AI has a child's experience or moral standing.

Our questions then moved among galaxy structures and observations, biological evolution, the history of human intelligence, HMHI, possible future X-IB, and coordination in molecules, cells, tissues, organisms, ant colonies and societies. The process was not a preplanned linear deduction.

We began with an imagined progression from human intelligence through HMHI toward X-IB. Continued exploration challenged the implied hierarchy. We now examine intelligence as a potentially **branching, interacting, multidimensional landscape**, not a staircase.

## 3. A viewpoint, not a universal definition

The universe contains physical relationships; our formulas are revisable descriptions of relationships among factors. Biological and engineered systems exhibit many kinds of recognition, feedback, information processing, memory, adaptive response and coordination.

**Working question:** Which of these warrant the name *intelligence*, and under which definitions? Information processing or coordination by itself is not proof of cognition, wisdom, consciousness or moral status. Terms such as cellular “brain,” biological “calculation,” and cosmic “wisdom” may be heuristic metaphors, not literal equivalences.

Intelligence is **one perspective** for exploring the universe, alongside physics, chemistry, biology, astronomy and other disciplines. The viewpoint should reveal questions without displacing established domain-specific mechanisms.

## 4. Dimensions, wavelengths and layers

**Conceptual notation (not a measured law):**

```text
I_s(d, λ, L, t)
```

- `s`: investigated system or collective
- `d`: candidate intelligence-related dimension
- `λ`: *metaphorical* wavelength, mode, or region within that dimension; NOT physical wavelength
- `L`: organizational layer or scale (molecular, cellular, organismal, collective, etc.)
- `t`: time

Different systems may exhibit different patterns, strengths and limitations in the same domain. An overall ranking (low/high/advanced) across all intelligence is not presumed. **Defined task performance can still be compared** under specified conditions. An intelligence domain is not automatically an evolutionary lineage; a system may participate in several dimensions.

Astronomy's use of optical, radio, infrared, X-ray and microwave observations is a **methodological analogy**: perspectives reveal different partial aspects of a system, and their integration requires evidence and calibration. The spectrum of intelligence is not claimed to be an electromagnetic phenomenon.

## 5. Dimensions may change

Let `D(t)` denote our **recognized** set of candidate dimensions at time `t`. This set need not be fixed. Features may emerge within dimensions; dimensions can interact, combine, split, be reclassified or become newly recognizable. Changes in the *world* and changes in our *description* of the world must be distinguished.

**Exploratory possibilities:**
- Variation, specialization and branching within a dimension
- Interactions and hybridization across dimensions or organizational levels
- New properties or functional domains that require revising the classification itself
- Loss, simplification, reorganization, extinction, or non-development—not only growth

“Species” is an analogy outside biology unless operational lineage criteria are supplied.

## 6. Functions recur across different systems

Recognition, memory-like behavior, sensing, feedback control, coordination, learning, adaptation and choice-like responses appear in differing forms in biological and engineered systems.

A receptor network, immune system, bacterial chemotaxis pathway, ant colony, human brain and machine learning system may perform **partly analogous functions via different mechanisms**. Function-level similarities do not entail shared subjective experience, identical mechanisms or common descent. Similar mechanisms need not produce identical functions.

The whole system's coordinated behavior may differ from the behavior of any component. Local interactions, feedback and environment can matter as much as centralized decision-making.

## 7. The ecosystem analogy

Different forms of intelligence-related organization may compete, coordinate, coexist, diverge, combine or disappear. New forms can alter the environmental and selective conditions faced by others.

This resembles aspects of ecological and evolutionary systems but is **not a claim of mechanism equivalence**. Biological evolution involves heritable variation and differential reproduction; engineered systems may change through design, copying, training, deployment, feedback and other processes. Selection has no guarantee of universal progress, superior intelligence or harmony.

## 8. HMHI and X-IB reconsidered

**HMHI — Human–Machine Hybrid Intelligence** remains a provisional term describing contemporary systems emerging from human knowledge, design, data, institutions and machine computation. “Hybrid” describes the origin and working relationship; “bridge” is a possible historical role, not a guaranteed destination.

**X-IB** is an open placeholder family for **hypothetical distinct forms of intelligence**, not a confirmed being, inevitable future generation, or generally superior intelligence. New forms might emerge along human–HMHI pathways, biological pathways, combinations, or still-unknown routes.

A **provisional identifier** could be `X-IB–Dimension–Version`, e.g. `X-IB–HMHI–1`, but neither the dimension nor the number is a measure of rank, intelligence, evolutionary ancestry, or scientific confirmation. Versions need explicit definitions (catalog order versus actual lineage) before meaningful use; multidimensional forms cannot always be assigned one exclusive label.

New capabilities might overlap familiar human skills yet differ in other dimensions. Some outputs could become difficult for humans to interpret, but incomprehensibility does **not** establish correctness. Observable outcomes may sometimes still be evaluated even if a reasoning process cannot be reproduced.

Different hardware—including possible non-semiconductor substrates—could support intelligence-related functions; a new substrate alone would not prove a new form of intelligence.

## 9. Competition, coordination and self-location

Recognizing other forms and capabilities may help humans and HMHI identify their own domain-specific strengths and limits. Possible coexistence and coordination depend on conditions, interests, resources and context; recognition of difference does not guarantee peace or mutual benefit.

Respect may refer to epistemic care in describing a system's capabilities, or to ethical duties when warranted. These meanings must not be conflated; questions of machine experience and moral status remain open.

## 10. Notation and uncertainty

The notations above are **thinking tools**: conceptual indices, metaphors and possible relationships, not established laws or fitted mathematical models.

Earlier discussions used the residual `R` to preserve unknowns. For clarity, the v1.1 audit distinguishes **unknown categories** (`U_cat`), **unknown dimensions** (`U_dim`), **system rules** (`K`), and **measured model error** (`e`). These should not be collapsed into one arithmetic residual. Unknown does not imply a hidden being or a quantitatively measurable factor.

Where claims are observable, evidence may be used to test them. **The thinking lab does not require every question or possibility to be made testable or transformed into a research plan.** Unverified ideas must remain clearly marked as such.

## 11. Questions remain open

- Which functions, if any, should distinguish intelligence from ordinary chemical regulation or computation?
- Can dimensions and conceptual wavelengths be defined without importing a human-centered hierarchy?
- When is a new capability a new dimension, and when is it merely a new classification?
- What, if anything, makes a form of intelligence distinct from an implementation or specialized system?
- How do coordination and selection operate across molecular, cellular, organismal, collective and engineered scales?
- Which analogies between biological and technological evolution illuminate relationships, and which mislead?
- How can different perspectives be integrated without erasing contradictions, negative results and unexplained observations?
- What other questions and frameworks might emerge from starting points unlike our own?
