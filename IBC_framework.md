# IBC — Intelligent Beings: Competition and Coordination

## Working snapshot v1.1 — notation revision

IBC is an open question space. It offers provisional language, analogies and hypotheses, not a finished theory or established natural laws. This revision clarifies the mathematical status of v1; it does not supply empirical validation.

The [original v1](IBC_v1.md) remains a historical snapshot. The [notation audit](NOTATION_AUDIT.md) maps its expressions to their assumptions and replacements. The [notation guide](NOTATION_GUIDE.md) provides definitions and a bounded example of possible future testing.

Every expression is labeled:

- **[D] Definition:** a stipulated term or description convention.
- **[M] Metaphor:** an analogy, scenario or workflow; no arithmetic or causal law implied.
- **[H] Hypothesis:** a conditional claim that may fail.
- **[MM candidate] Measurable-model candidate:** a specified proposal for measurement and prediction, requiring validation.
- **[O] Open placeholder:** an unresolved question or property, not a detected entity or measured quantity.

Labels can coexist. Definitions do not establish existence; mathematical notation does not establish evidence. Project principles and normative judgments are identified separately.

## 1. The naming question

**[M]** A ladder from human intelligence to AI to superintelligence is one way of imagining intelligence, not an inevitable trajectory. IBC asks whether heterogeneous, overlapping capabilities provide a more useful question space. “Spectrum” and “wavelength” are metaphors; they imply no physical wavelength, continuous axis or total ranking.

## 2. Candidate beings, systems and categories

**[D/O]** Intelligent Beings is a provisional umbrella, whose inclusion criteria remain open. A label does not settle intelligence, consciousness, agency or moral status.

\[
\mathcal L_v:=\{\mathrm{human},\mathrm{nonhuman\ animal},\mathrm{cellular},\mathrm{HMHI}\},
\qquad \ell_v(b;\Pi)\subseteq\mathcal L_v.
\]

Here b is a candidate entity or system, v is the framework version, and Π states inclusion criteria and the level of analysis. Labels are nonexclusive: cellular systems can occur within animals, and joint human–machine systems require explicit boundaries. A classification can be unresolved. The cellular label opens a question about intelligence-like behavior rather than declaring an established equivalence with human intelligence.

**[O]** Unknown category questions are recorded in U_cat,v. This register replaces the category use of R. It is not an enumerated set of all unknown beings and is not itself a being.

## 3. HMHI

**[D]** HMHI means Human–Machine Hybrid Intelligence, the project's provisional term for present machine-implemented AI. The naming choice remains revisable.

**[M/H]** Human knowledge, architecture, data of varied provenance, training, feedback and computation are possible contributors to AI development. They are not additive quantities. Human-derived provenance is distinct from active human–machine collaboration; each case must state which meaning is intended. Neither meaning proves that human and machine cognition share an internal implementation.

## 4. Describing a being without assuming all its properties

**[D/O]** Intelligence is a proposed attribute of a being or system, not a mathematical subset of that entity.

\[
P_{b,v}:=(D_{b,v},M_{b,\Pi},\sigma_{b,v},\mathcal U_{\mathrm{dim},b,v}).
\]

D contains verbal descriptions; M contains operational measures actually available; σ records property status; U_dim records unresolved property questions. Possible fields include intelligence, capability, affect, wisdom and preferences. Listing a field does not assert that the property exists. Unknown, not assessed, and justified as not applicable are different statuses; none is zero. No common units, independent coordinates or exhaustive property list is assumed.

## 5. Spectrum and measured profiles

**[M]** “Intelligence spectrum” suggests heterogeneous capabilities, possible overlap and possible complementarity. It is not a measurable spectrum as presently defined.

**[D; operational only when a protocol exists]**

\[
p_b(d,e,t;\Pi):=\text{recorded performance under protocol }\Pi.
\]

Specify task/domain d, environment e and time t. Performance is not a complete measure of intelligence or a being. Group profiles may differ on some tasks and overlap on others; differences are empirical questions, not universal inequalities. Do not combine scores without a justified aggregation rule. Preserve unassessed domains in U_dim rather than inventing an additive unknown-intelligence remainder.

## 6. Task substitution and being replacement

**[D/H]** Performance on a defined task does not by itself establish replacement of an entity in a specified system and time horizon. Specialization, overlap, coexistence and displacement are possible scenarios, not an exhaustive list or predetermined outcome. Their occurrence requires domain-specific evidence.

## 7. Competition, coordination and future systems

**[D]** Comp and Coord denote interaction concepts. They are not physical forces or automatically scalar quantities. Lowercase c_comp and c_coord are reserved for indicators defined by a measurement protocol. They need not sum to one.

**[H1/O]** Within a specified system and horizon, competition and coordination may help explain defined outcomes, conditional on context. The unrestricted expression “Future = F(Competition, Coordination, …)” is replaced by this question because its output, inputs and function were unspecified.

Competition may promote experimentation or suppress it; coordination may enable division of labor or suppress diversity. Each proposed mechanism needs separate testing. Neither process is assumed to improve a chosen outcome monotonically. “Best” requires a stakeholder, criterion, feasible alternatives and horizon.

## 8. Observation and inquiry

**[M]** Observing, proposing patterns, summarizing and asking new questions form an iterative research workflow. A pattern can fail and send the inquiry back to observation. A workflow is not validation. Competition, coordination, rules, learning and adaptation are illustrative topics, not an exhaustive explanation of system behavior.

## 9. Specify the system

**[D]**

\[
\mathcal S_v:=(M_v,\partial_v,J_v,K_v,\{O_{a,v}\}_{a\in M_v},T_v).
\]

Record members M, boundary ∂, interactions J, rules/constraints K, actor-specific objectives O and horizon T, together with analyst and inclusion criteria. Objectives can be unknown or conflicting. The tuple is a selected representation and can omit relevant features. K replaces the old rules symbol R; J avoids confusing interactions with intelligence.

**[H]** An observation of competition in one system does not establish the same relationship in another. Compare alternative boundaries instead of assuming one is uniquely correct.

## 10. Emergent interaction patterns

**[H]** Interests, rules, environment and feedback may influence competition and coordination. Effects can be reciprocal, and omitted variables may matter. Emergence and central planning are possibilities to investigate rather than an assumed dichotomy. Causal arrows require a separately specified model and evidence.

## 11. Disagreement, conflict and hostility

**[D]** Different opinions, core-interest conflict, competition and hostility are distinct concepts that can coexist. None should be treated as automatically entailing another.

**[H2]** Under stated incentives and power relations, specified rules and enforcement may alter the probability of bounded competition. Shared rules alone do not guarantee it. Alternatives include conflict, avoidance and rule violation.

## 12. Coordination modes

**[D/O]** Describe coordination with labels such as emergent, intentional, mixed or undetermined. Passive and proactive components are not assumed to be exclusive, exhaustive, commensurable or additive. Intentionality may be difficult to establish.

**[M/H]** Adaptation followed by a growing problem and later deliberate coordination is one candidate pathway. Early coordination, persistent conflict, no response and collapse are alternatives. A threshold is not presumed universal.

## 13. Shared threat

**[M]** “Lazy systems” is an analogy for persistence, not an explanation or a property ascribed to every system.

**[H3]** Within a prespecified threat range and context, perceived shared threat may be associated with a change in the probability of a defined coordination event. The direction, size and shape are open to testing. Severe threat can also impair coordination. Use z_threat for a measured threat indicator; T remains time horizon. Resources, trust and perception may modify the relationship. Association does not establish causality.

## 14. Changing boundaries

**[D/H]** Humanity is one possible analytical system boundary, not automatically a unified decision-maker. Distinguish an analyst's boundary from the boundary salient to each participant at a given time. Whether shared threat changes those boundaries or interaction patterns is a testable question in defined settings.

## 15. Capability, intelligence and wisdom

**[D/O]** Provisional meanings are: capability—what an entity can do; intelligence—abilities such as learning, reasoning, prediction and adaptation; wisdom—judgment about what, when, why and whether something should be done. These constructs may overlap, and wisdom requires explicit evaluative criteria.

Use A_b for capability when a symbol is needed, avoiding C for both capability and competition/coordination. An increase in a capability measure does not by itself establish increases in intelligence or wisdom measures. An intelligence gain does not by itself establish a wisdom gain. This does not claim statistical independence.

## 16. Emotion

**[D/O]** Emotion recognition performance and subjective emotional experience are different evidential targets. Record them separately. No machine affective experience is inferred from fluent emotional output or a listed property field. The status of experience and appropriate evidence remain open.

## 17. Curiosity

**[H/O]** Exploration may occur without immediate survival pressure. Compare novelty seeking, information seeking, reward, instrumental need and other explanations. Curiosity-like behavior does not alone establish subjective curiosity or independence from all incentives. Unknown situations can prompt avoidance as well as exploration.

## 18. Curiosity and coordination

**[M/H]** Questions, different views, exploration and revision describe a possible dialogue workflow, not a guaranteed sequence. A hypothesis that curiosity supports low-friction coordination requires measures of curiosity, friction, conflict, participation and outcome quality. “Smooth” also needs a criterion and does not establish correctness.

## 19. Generation and verification

**Project practice / [M]:** Encourage open generation followed by explicit verification, with iteration and preserved negative results. Respectful disagreement remains compatible with rigorous criticism.

**[H]** Effects of good faith, openness or positive attitude on creativity and validity require separate tests. A preferred working style is not evidence of better results.

## 20. Human values

**[M/O]** Biology, history, society and interests are possible influences on human values, not additive quantities. Values vary among humans and contexts. V_a,context can describe stated actor values if defined. A universe-level value system is not operationally specified; neither equality nor inequality with human values is established. Philosophical and normative questions remain distinct from empirical claims.

## 21. Evaluation

**[D]**

\[
Q_q(y;\mathcal S,a,T):=\text{evaluation of outcome }y\text{ under rule }q.
\]

Specify stakeholder a, system S, horizon T and rule q before interpreting “good,” “effective” or “successful.” Report evidence uncertainty separately using justified intervals, distributions or an explicit unknown status. A chosen evaluation rule is not a natural law or a universally shared value.

## 22. Diversity, coordination and domination

**[D]** Coordination and uniformity are different concepts; one does not necessarily entail the other.

**[H4]** Defined diversity and interaction patterns may affect specified resilience or adaptation outcomes. The direction and boundary conditions remain empirical questions. “Healthier” is evaluative until its criterion is stated; diversity, competition and coordination are not an additive recipe for health.

## 23. Consciousness, agency and moral status

**[D/O]** Intelligence, consciousness, agency and moral status are distinct constructs. IBC treats no one as sufficient evidence for another. This does not establish independence or absence of relationships. Rights and decision authority involve normative questions as well as factual evidence. Their resolution is not supplied by category membership or task performance.

## 24. Economic scenarios

**[M/H]** A labor-saving scenario holds output or demand conditions sufficiently fixed that automation can reduce labor requirements. A complementarity scenario links collaboration to capabilities, products, demand and economic activity. Neither sequence is inevitable. Each link needs evidence, a boundary and a horizon. Failed adoption, displacement, demand constraints and coordination costs remain possible.

The inquiry is: what can humans and HMHI together do under defined conditions, at what cost, and with what effects for different participants?

## 25. R&D workflows

**[M]** Questions, machine-assisted exploration, experiments, observations and interpretation can form an iterative workflow. Include provenance, independent verification, failed experiments, negative results and stopping decisions. The diagram of a workflow would not itself demonstrate scientific benefit. Robotics can be an additional component whose contribution also needs evaluation.

## 26. The originating dialogue

**Reported case / [M]:** The project describes a dialogue involving questions, alternative views, disagreement, reconstruction and revision. Preserve time-ordered raw exchanges, including typos, failed formulations and unresolved disagreements, if evaluating that claim.

A naturalistic case can generate hypotheses. It cannot alone establish a causal collaboration advantage or estimate separable synergy and coordination loss. The guide proposes a matched-performance comparison for future study; no such result is claimed here.

## 27. The broader picture

**[M/H]** An ecosystem of interacting entities is one possible scenario, not a prediction of universal coexistence. A chain of arrows among humans, animals, cells, HMHI, R and environment is not an established interaction topology.

**[D; case-specific]** If a graph is useful, define G := (N,E_obs), with explicitly selected entities and dated, typed evidence for each observed edge. Keep hypothesized edges separate. Unknown category questions belong in U_cat, not in the graph as an interacting being. Direction, reciprocity and strength must not be presumed.

## 28. Limits of the framework

IBC does not establish that a particular future must occur, that coordination is always beneficial, that competition should disappear, or that all candidates are equivalent. It does not establish machine consciousness, subjective emotion, wisdom or rights. Definitions and metaphors are thinking tools. Hypotheses require evidence; candidate models require operationalization and validation.

## 29. Open questions and future candidates

**[O]** Definitions of intelligent being, unknown categories and dimensions, generalization across substrates, and relationships among competition, coordination, curiosity and wisdom remain open. Preserve unexplained observations rather than forcing them into a preferred account.

**Proposed scenario extension, not part of public v1 [O]:** X-IB_j may name an imagined or hypothesized candidate beyond the current classification. An index identifies a scenario, not a detected being, ranked generation or guaranteed future stage. Existence, substrate, autonomy, curiosity, consciousness, goals and rights remain separate unresolved properties. X-IB scenarios do not exhaust U_cat. See the notation guide for optional notation.

## 30. Provisional definition of IBC

**[D]** IBC is an exploratory framework for asking how candidate intelligent beings and systems interact through competition, coordination and other processes within explicitly selected boundaries. Its vocabulary and descriptions are revisable; it does not predict a predetermined future. Descriptions, hypotheses, evidence, value judgments and unresolved questions must remain distinguishable.

## 31. Public-release principle

**Project principle:** Protect the question, not the proposal. Retain prior snapshots and reasons for changes. Preserve uncertainty and unexplained residual patterns. A public version is a working snapshot, not a declaration of completion.

## 32. An open lab for thinking

**Project principle:** Invite criticism, alternative definitions, missing dimensions, counterexamples, boundary changes and entirely different models. The dialogue–revision–new-question sequence is a process ideal, not a guaranteed source of truth. The preference for exploration over ownership is a value commitment, not a mathematical inequality. The project is useful if it helps people formulate worthwhile new questions while remaining able to reject its proposals.
